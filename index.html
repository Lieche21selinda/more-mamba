The academic

Teaching law as a way of thinking, not a body of rules

This portfolio sets out how I teach, how I assess, how I build a classroom that works for every student in it, and what my students have taught me in return.

My teaching sits across two levels and two campuses. In IURI271 Criminal Procedure, at Potchefstroom, I work with undergraduates on the rules governing how the state investigates, arrests, charges and tries a person — the point at which constitutional rights either hold or fail in practice. In PPLM824 Public International Law, at Mahikeng, I work with a master's cohort on questions that are live in the world right now: the reach of the International Court of Justice, the limits of the Security Council, the regulation of technologies that arrived faster than the statutes governing them.

What connects the two is a conviction that runs through every slide that follows: law students learn most when the law in front of them is unfinished. My job is to put them in the room where the argument is still open, and to give them the tools to take a position in it.

Criterion 3

Inclusive teaching practices

How I build a learning environment that reduces barriers rather than working around them, guided by a framework and evidenced in what students actually do.

To write — inclusive teaching practices

  • Outline the strategies you use to support diversity, with evidence of how each is enacted.
  • Show how your teaching keeps all students included and actively engaged.
  • Articulate how you tailor learning to promote equity, and how that lifts engagement and performance.
  • Name the framework guiding you — Universal Design for Learning is one option — and link it back to your teaching statement.
  • The top band asks for the multiple ways learning takes place, and for barriers to be reduced rather than accommodated.
1 000 words · udlguidelines.cast.org

To write — student voice

To write — and this is where Criterion 4 is won

The survey asked what would improve the module. Students named specific things:

  • IURI271 — more visual learning tools: flowcharts, short explainer videos, step-by-step guides for hard concepts like jurisdiction and the use of force; topic summaries before readings; simulations or mini-moots.
  • IURI271 — more practice questions, and guidance on how to answer a given question type.
  • PPLM824 — small group tutorials, peer-to-peer case analyses, debates on international disputes.

Say what you changed in response, and what happened next. The top band rewards feedback that visibly reshaped planning, not feedback that was merely collected.
